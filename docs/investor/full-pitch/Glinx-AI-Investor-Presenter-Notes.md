# Glinx AI: Full Investor Pitch

September 2026. 21 main slides and 4 reference slides.

Present the main story in approximately 12–15 minutes. Use the appendix for detailed questions.

## 1. Glinx AI: the self-improving company

Glinx has one main directive: make a company self-aware so it can recursively self-improve. In business terms, this means a persistent model of the company that understands its purpose and current condition, proposes changes, and learns from measured results. The owner defines the objectives and permissions. The full platform is a product vision. Today, an installable Company Discovery prototype establishes the first sensing layer. The cover illustration depicts a fictional manufacturing environment and does not represent a customer installation.

## 2. The Glinx investment thesis

The investment thesis combines a large ambition with a narrow starting point. Companies already hold operating information in ERP, email, finance and other systems. Glinx would preserve the evidence and connect it to business meaning, then use outcomes to update the next decision. The initial manufacturing focus is a customer hypothesis, supported by the founder’s operating experience. No paying customer, executed pilot, measured savings or financing amount is claimed. The next milestone is customer evidence that a complete loop can improve a real decision.

## 4. Company self-awareness

Self-awareness here means a functional business capability, not consciousness or legal personhood. The company model needs explicit objectives, a current state, evidence for its explanations and memory over time. It should know the limits of its knowledge. Human leaders continue to resolve trade-offs among profit, service, quality, safety and other constraints. Recursive improvement means feeding evaluated outcomes into later cycles. It does not mean unconstrained self-modification or permission to pursue a metric at any cost.

## 6. The Glinx operating architecture

The founder’s reference image defines five layers. Company organs are the source systems, including ERP, email, finance, HR and tax. Connector fabric acquires permitted records using APIs, change-data capture, files or events. The evidence plane preserves original records, lineage and permissions. The Company Connectome resolves business entities, relationships and meaning. Glinx cognition diagnoses, simulates, acts and learns. The outcome of an action must return as new evidence for the next cycle. Most of this end-state architecture remains to be built. The current prototype identifies approved system metadata and records its evidence.

## 8. The recursive self-improvement loop

This loop operationalizes the main directive. Sense refreshes evidence. Understand forms explanations with uncertainty. Decide compares options against owner-defined objectives and constraints. Act executes only authorized changes. Measure evaluates results against a baseline, including unintended effects. Learn retains or rejects the change and updates the model, playbook or monitoring criteria. An agent may launch a narrowly scoped subtask only under a pre-approved policy with bounded access, budget and duration. It must not grant itself authority, rewrite its objectives or blindly reinforce its earlier answer. Consequential actions require human approval. The full loop is a future product capability.

## 9. One late-order problem can become a learning cycle

Walk through one concrete example rather than claiming the platform can optimize everything at once. A manufacturing owner sees worsening delivery performance and rising overtime. The proposed system brings together dated records and tests plausible explanations. It compares a bounded intervention with alternatives, and a person approves the change. It then measures delivery, cost and quality over an agreed window. The next cycle starts with an improved hypothesis or a rejected intervention, not a claim that any observed improvement proves causation. Native ERP access, reasoning, action execution and outcome evaluation remain future work.

## 10. Who would use Glinx

The proposed buyer is the owner, CEO or general manager responsible for the company’s performance. An operations leader champions the first use case. Finance and process owners validate definitions, operating evidence and the value calculation. IT and security approve integration boundaries and controls. The proposed 100–499 employee manufacturing segment is a starting focus, not a validated market segment or an exclusion of smaller companies. A firm needs enough workflow complexity to justify integration effort, a named owner and accessible source records. Longer-term users could include distribution and service businesses and operating teams managing multiple companies.

## 12. The Glinx product portfolio

This is the broader Glinx product map. Know combines Discovery with the proposed persistent company model. Diagnose would explain problems with traceable evidence and uncertainty. Prioritize would rank opportunities using costs, expected impact and risk assumptions. Plan would translate a chosen opportunity into an accountable roadmap. Operate and learn would use scoped agents to carry out permitted tasks and evaluate their effects. The architecture would allow appropriate models and tools for each task. Glinx does not need to train its own foundation model to test this thesis. Today’s inventory questions use deterministic rules, and the full reasoning and execution capabilities remain planned.

## 16. Manufacturing offers a focused starting market

NAM reports 239,265 U.S. manufacturing firms in 2022, with 93.1% below 100 employees and 4,177 firms at 500 or more. Approximate firms at 100–499 employees = 239,265 × (1 − 0.931) − 4,177 = 12,332.285. Because the share is rounded, the deck rounds the pool down to about 12,000 rather than claiming precision. The scenario multiplies 12,000 firms by an assumed $60,000 annual subscription to obtain $720 million. This is a gross potential pool for a selected segment, not a validated TAM, SAM, forecast or evidence of willingness to pay. Filtering for data access, workflow fit, budget, geography and channels will reduce the reachable market. ## 18. Defensibility depends on accumulated evidence

The moat hypothesis has four parts: a useful company-specific model, a decision-and-outcome history, reusable deployment work and customer trust in operating controls. None is a current proven moat. Glinx should keep customer records exportable and permissioned. Reusable schemas, code, tests and appropriately authorized patterns can improve delivery without pooling confidential customer data by default. An advantage would show up in declining deployment effort, greater repeat usage, better-calibrated recommendations and retained customers. A generic model wrapper or a large number of connectors alone would be easy for competitors to match.

## 22. Governance must constrain the improvement loop

Governance is part of the proposed product architecture. Owners set goals and constraints. Data access needs source-native authorization, tenant isolation and auditability. Agents should receive only the tools and time needed for a task, with explicit budgets and controlled delegation. Untrusted documents and emails must remain data rather than agent instructions. Financial commitments, employment decisions, sensitive disclosures and material operational changes require designated human authority and specialist review where appropriate. Versioned workflows, evaluations, stop controls and tested recovery are necessary before deployment. The current metadata-only prototype does not implement the full production control framework.

## 23. Customer economics depend on the verified gain

This sensitivity is an arithmetic illustration, not a customer case, savings commitment or pricing proposal accepted by the founder. It assumes a $3 million annual addressable cost base. A 2%, 5% or 8% recurring reduction yields $60,000, $150,000 or $240,000 annual gross benefit. Assumed year-one cost is $60,000 software plus $30,000 implementation, totaling $90,000. Year-one net benefits are therefore negative $30,000, positive $60,000 and positive $150,000. No timing ramp, taxes or financing effects are modeled. The low case demonstrates that a technically working tool may still fail economically. Cash released from working capital is a separate benefit and must not be counted as recurring cost savings. Agreement on baseline, attribution and adverse effects is essential.


## 24. The investment case has clear risks to test

Five risks could invalidate the investment thesis: integration remains bespoke, the company model cannot reliably resolve context, measured outcomes reflect confounding or metric gaming, customers will not pay enough to support the product, or incumbents deliver an easier alternative. A pilot should collect disconfirming evidence as well as positive examples. A useful company model must identify missing coverage and contradictory records. The team should define minimum evidence and guardrails before an intervention and preserve failed experiments. The business needs evidence of retention and repeatability, not only demonstrations. These risks are Glinx planning judgments rather than externally sourced estimates.
